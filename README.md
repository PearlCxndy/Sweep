# Sweep

Shopping-list apps are built for the two minutes before the shop. Sweep is
built for the forty minutes inside it.

Five screens plus a finish screen. Three of shopping, two of everything else.
That ratio is the point.

## The data is invented

Everything in `src/data/seed/` is generated by `scripts/seed.ts`. There is no
retailer feed behind it, no retailer relationship, and no claim of either.
Aisle numbers, prices, stock and purchase history are fabricated to demonstrate
the product's logic.

```
npm run seed     # regenerate, and print the counts it produced
```

The generator is anchored to a fixed reference date (2026-08-23) so the
committed output is reproducible. It prints what it made:

```
store          Tesco Express, 262 Poplar High St (14 seeded aisles)
products       51
trips          22  (2026-02-14 .. 2026-08-15)
purchases      446
milk           31 purchases, median 9 days
own-brand      88 staple purchases
basket overlap 87% big shop to big shop
basket sizes   big 29, top-up 6
```

## Running it

```
npm install
npm run dev      # http://localhost:3000
npm test         # domain logic only: suggestions, ranking, safety filter
npm run build
```

To see the offline behaviour, put the device in airplane mode part-way through
a trip. The trip is in `localStorage` and every action is queued with the time
it happened, so nothing waits on the network. The word `offline` appears in
mono. There is no warning banner.

## The walkthrough

1. **Your list** — suggestions at the top, then eleven items grouped by aisle.
2. **Start shopping** — the ground inverts to ink, one item at a time.
3. **Not here** on the Tesco semi-skimmed 2L — up to three substitute tickets,
   one recommended.
4. **Not here** on the Free From white loaf — no alternatives at all, because
   the shopper protected it. Her note is shown in her own words.
5. **That's the shop** — counts, and one question, asked only because a swap
   happened.

Both out-of-stock beats are rigged in `stock.json` so the walkthrough fires
reliably. Trip mode shows a `SEEDED DEMO` line on those two items. That line is
scaffolding for the case study and would not ship — Sweep never claims to know
what is on a shelf, because the shopper is the one who can see it.

## Trip mode

Three things stop one-item-at-a-time from being a cage.

**The route rail opens into the list, at two grains.** Nobody walks the route
we drew. They come in the far door, or wander into aisle 6 first, and reordering
a list one-handed with a trolley in the other is the wrong answer to that.

*Tap a line* to take the one thing you are looking at — it stages immediately,
with an `OUT OF ORDER` banner back to the route. Nothing is moved, so nothing
has to be moved back, and a line already in the trolley reopens the same way,
where the primary action becomes **Take it back out**.

*Tap an aisle heading* — `START HERE` — to say "I'm in aisle 6", which is the
answer when there are three things in aisle 6 and jumping to each in turn is
just tedium. That is a **rotation, not a reshuffle**: the walk begins at that
aisle, carries on in store order, and the aisles already behind the shopper
keep their own order and come round at the end. Because nothing moves relative
to anything else within its half, the rotation is reversible — `PUT IT BACK`
clears one field and the original walk returns, with no record of the detour to
unpick.

Two fields carry all of it. `Trip.startFrom` holds the aisle the walk begins
at, and `orderTripItems` rotates on it by sorting on a lap flag before the walk
index — so `items` stays in walking order, which is the invariant everything
else reads. In the screen, `focusId` covers both resolving a gap on the shelf
and jumping ahead, because they are the same idea — the screen follows the
shopper, not the sort order — and it clears the moment the item settles, or the
moment the route admits where the shopper is standing.

**Swipe is a shortcut, never the only path.** Right for In the trolley, left
for Not here. The buttons do not move or shrink to make room: a shopper in
gloves, or with a child on one arm, taps. The gesture locks its axis once and
holds it, and a drag that starts vertical is handed straight back to the
browser — fighting the scroller over an ambiguous gesture is how a screen
starts feeling broken halfway down an aisle.

**The trolley total runs along the top.** `basket()` sums by status, so a swap
counts once at the price of the thing actually picked up and a skipped line is
worth nothing to either figure. Prices come from a catalogue and not from the
till, so lines with no price are counted separately and shown rather than
quietly dropped — `£23.40+` in the header, spelled out in the rail. The figure
lands again on the finish screen, because a gauge that vanishes reads as one
that was never counting.

One animation was added to the one that existed. Tapping In the trolley commits
the state immediately and the next item rises into place; a *copy* of the mark
lifts, tips and falls out of frame on its own layer, while the progress segment
it earned snaps and settles. Nothing waits for the flourish. Reduced motion
replaces all of it with a fade.

## Shape

```
src/domain/         pure functions, no React, no I/O
  suggestions.ts    replenishment prediction — median intervals, no model
  confidence.ts     evidence strength -> high | medium | low
  substitutions.ts  safety filter first, then ranking
  storeOrder.ts     aisle order, with a category fallback
  basket.ts         running trolley total, honest about unpriced lines
src/data/           repositories returning plain objects, over seeded JSON
src/components/     no component library
src/lib/            zustand store, offline queue, formatting
```

A product does not have an aisle globally. A product has an aisle **at a
store**, so `StorePlacement` is modelled separately from `Product`. The Free
From loaf is `category: "bakery"` and sits in aisle 10, Free From — which is
exactly the kind of thing that separation exists for.

`substitutesFor()` returns `[]` for a protected product. The safety filter is a
hard gate that runs before ranking, never a low score, and the tests assert
that ranking would otherwise have produced a result — so the empty return is
the gate and not an accident of scoring.

## Onboarding

Two mechanisms, different jobs.

**The first-run card** appears once, on first open of the list, and never
again. Three lines. It states the one genuinely non-obvious thing about Sweep —
that the interface changes shape when you start shopping — plus the two facts
that would otherwise be a surprise: the list populates itself, and a locked
item stays locked. One action, no skip, no dots, no swipe.

**Coach marks** are one line each, delivered where the control is, tracked
individually so a shopper who never hits a gap on a shelf still gets that
explanation the first time they do. They sit in the flow at the foot of the
screen rather than floating over it, and acting on the item dismisses them
without needing the button.

| id | fires |
| --- | --- |
| `trip_mode` | first item screen, 800ms in — let them see it first |
| `not_here` | first item staged as unavailable, before they tap |
| `swipe` | after one item is settled — a shortcut, offered once the buttons have been used |
| `locked_item` | first time the locked panel is reached |
| `dismiss_suggestion` | first suggestion turned down |
| `trip_complete` | first trip finished |

Nothing re-fires on update. A flow that changes enough to need re-explaining
gets a new coach mark id, not a reset of an old one.

### Three changes to the onboarding brief

**The coach mark copy spoke as an assistant.** "I'll suggest something close"
and "I'll stop offering that one" are first person, which the build spec's copy
rules forbid — and which the onboarding brief itself forbids two sections
earlier ("No 'I'll help you'"). Both lines are rewritten in the product's
voice.

**"Tick and it moves on" named a control that does not exist.** The button says
*In the trolley*, and the spec says actions keep their name through the flow.
The line now uses the words on the button.

**"Dismissal is feedback, not deletion" was not true of the code.** One
dismissal suppressed a suggestion for good, so the copy would have been a lie.
Dismissals now clear when the trip finishes, and three of them retire the
suggestion permanently — which is what the line promises, and what
`retiredByDismissals` tests.

The `not_here` mark is the one that pre-empts rather than follows, and it is
staged by the seeded stock flag. Sweep has no stock feed and never claims one;
shipping this would need a different trigger, because only the shopper can see
the shelf.

There is no store picker. The brief puts the card after one, but the seed has a
single store, so the card fires on first open of the list.

## The list as blocks

`domain/treemap.ts` packs the aisle groups into a rectangle, each block sized by
how many items that aisle holds. A fixed two-column grid left ragged space at
the bottom and gave a one-item aisle the same weight as a five-item one; the
treemap fills the rectangle exactly and makes the big aisles look big.

**It does not sort.** The published squarified algorithm orders descending for
the best aspect ratios. Sweep cannot: the list is in walking order, which is the
entire product, and sorting by size would put aisle 12 before aisle 2 and send
the shopper back on themselves. Input order is preserved and the aspect ratios
come out slightly worse. A tidier rectangle is not worth a wrong route.

With the seeded list — 11 items across 8 aisles at 350×437 — that gives 100%
coverage, aspect ratios between 1.02 and 1.82, and a smallest tile side of 88px
against a 44px minimum target. Dairy holds four items and reads as the largest
block at 36% of the area.

## The produce

`ProduceScene` is a matter.js scene, not an illustration. The fruit drops in,
knocks against itself and settles wherever it lands, so the pile is never quite
the same twice and never reads as a laid-out graphic. Once every body is asleep
the loop stops — this is scenery, not a running animation.

- Decorative and declared so: `aria-hidden`, `pointer-events: none`, and no
  mouse constraint, so it can never intercept a tap meant for the form on top.
- It sits on a negative layer inside an `isolate`d ground, which is what lets it
  paint above the background and behind every control.
- matter.js is imported dynamically, so it stays out of the first paint and off
  every route that does not draw produce.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` skips the fall: the same engine runs ninety silent
  steps and paints the settled result once.
- Fills come from what the product already uses — `ProductMark`, the Sweep start
  canvas, and `--ripe-strong`. No new greens, no new reds.

Half-slices are real polygons built with `Bodies.fromVertices`. That recentres
vertices on the centroid, so each slice carries a `drawOffset` and the canvas
shifts by it — without that the art sits visibly off its own body.

## Decisions taken against the spec

Three, stated rather than made quietly.

**The aisle numeral is paper on the ink ground, not `--wayfind`.** The spec
gives the aisle marker the wayfind colour. Wayfind on ink is about 2.5:1, and
the spec also requires trip mode to be legible at 60cm. The largest element in
the product has to win that. On paper grounds the marker keeps wayfind.

**No `--halt` on Screen 1.** The spec asks for a halt outline on the locked
item's group, and also says halt appears on exactly one screen. Both cannot
hold. The one-screen rule is in "do not change without saying so", so it wins:
a protected group on Screen 1 is marked with an ink rule and a mono label.
It is a one-line change in `src/app/page.tsx` if you want it the other way.

**Suggestions are scoped to the trip's store.** The signature takes a store; a
pattern at one shop is not evidence about another.

Two small additions to the types, both to serve rules the spec already states:
`Product.lineId` and `Product.attributes` (so "same product line" and "different
fat content" are data rather than string-matching), and `Inference.basis` — the
products a claim rests on, so that deleting a claim can actually take them out
of the inputs to suggestions rather than just hiding a row.

## Numbers in tension

The spec asks for 22 trips and milk every ~5 days. Twenty-two trips across six
months cannot produce a five-day cycle. The seed keeps 22 trips and 31 milk
purchases, and the inference reports the interval the data actually shows —
nine days. The system says what is true about the history it has.

Big shops are fortnightly Saturdays; top-ups are the Thursday between. Basket
overlap is measured big shop to big shop, because comparing a 29-item shop to a
6-item top-up measures the difference between two kinds of trip, not the
stability of a basket.

## Not built

Shared household lists, price comparison, barcode scanning, recipe browsing,
meal planning, budgeting, streaks, achievements, onboarding. Each is defensible
in v2. None serves the three failures.
